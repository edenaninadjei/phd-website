PhD Thesis

The Tie That Binds: Culture, Religion, and the Formation of Political Identity in British African Communities

To what extent do culture, religion, and generational status shape the political identity of British African voters? This thesis explores how political identities and behaviours are influenced by the political cultures of countries of origin and how these are renegotiated within the British context. Focusing on British Ghanaians and Nigerians, it examines how cultural norms, religious beliefs, and political cultures of countries of origin inform identity formation, political behaviour, and voting patterns across generations within the diaspora. Drawing on original focus group discussions and survey data, I argue that political identities among British African communities are neither fully retained from the home-state nor entirely assimilated into the host society. Instead, they are actively adapted and reconstructed, producing hybrid forms of political belonging shaped by both transnational ties and the lived experiences of race, migration, and citizenship in the UK. By centring African diasporic experiences, this study contributes to broader debates on diaspora politics, political socialisation, and cultural hybridity, offering new insights into how migrant communities negotiate identity and political engagement across borders and generations.
